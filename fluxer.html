stop handing over your id
to every single website




this page exists because something changed.


communication platforms used to connect people.
now they profile them.




> what fluxer is


fluxer is a community-first, open source alternative to platforms like discord. its designed to be ad-free, privacy-focused, community-funded, and self-hostable.

all the familiar features are already built, along with some new stuff:

- text channels
- voice & video
- roles & moderation
- custom emojis & theming
- self-hosting
- no ads
- no biometric ID pipeline
- AGPL-licensed source code
- and more on the way.

it works like the tools you're used to. servers, channels, DMs, etc. but without binding your identity to intelligence infrastructure.




> what happened to discord


discord is rolling out mandatory age verification in certain regions.
this can include:

- facial scans (biometric data)
- government ID submission, and
- 3rd party identity processors

the stated goal is “child safety.” however, the practical result is biometric data collection, permanent identity linkage, normalization of ID-gated speech, and outsourcing trust to opaque 3rd party vendors. once your face or ID enters these systems, control is no longer yours. even if data is “deleted,” embeddings, ai models, and logs can be left behind.

“temporary verification” does not mean temporary storage.
identity once correlated is identity forever correlated.




> “think of the kids” is not a blank check


every expansion of surveillance has used the same justification: terrorism, crime, misinformation, and... protecting minors.
the logic always sounds reasonable in isolation, but when platforms require government ID to speak freely, the very right of free speech comes under very real threat, for everyone.

it shifts society toward algorithmic social scoring, behavioral modeling at scale, and identity-based profiteering. this is the same logic as “if you have nothing to hide, you have nothing to fear.”
that argument does not protect freedom, rather, it dissolves it.


privacy is not about hiding wrongdoing.
it is about preserving agency.




> why fluxer matters


fluxer and similar projects reject the premise that communication requires biometric surrender. they offer a different path forward - one where communities can thrive without sacrificing their privacy or autonomy to do so.

a world where speech is not undermined by profit, where moderation is transparent and accountable, and where users can choose their own trust models (and hosts), is not only possible - but necessary to preserve the open internet as we know it.

fluxer is not (just) about nostalgia. it's about refusing to normalize a future where a right to free speech is trampled in favor of authoritarian compliance.

an open community does not promise perfection.
and, in many ways, thats part of the point.




> bring back the (open) internet


the internet was meant to be for expression, knowledge, and connection, built on the principles of openness, novelty, and community. but, as more and more of our online lives are ruled by platforms and algorithms that prioritize profit over people,

we risk losing the very essence of what made the internet special in the first place.

bring back:
- communities built on shared interest, not advertisements and profit
- moderation by humans, not opaque models
- identity by choice, not requirement
- speech without permission or biometrics
- privacy as the default, not unobtanium.




> conclusion


i don't want my face tied to my right to speak.
i don't want my passport attached to my hobbies.
i don't want corporate vendors deciding who gets access to community.

safety matters, so does freedom. if protecting people requires building permanent surveillance infrastructure, the cost is too high. we need to build a better way, as a community, and for the community.
